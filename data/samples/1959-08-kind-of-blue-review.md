Recorded over two sessions in March and April 1959 at Columbia's 30th Street Studio in New York, *Kind of Blue* gathers Miles Davis (trumpet), John Coltrane (tenor saxophone), Cannonball Adderley (alto saxophone), Bill Evans (piano), Paul Chambers (bass) and Jimmy Cobb (drums). Wynton Kelly replaces Evans at the piano on a single track, "Freddie Freeloader".

The record is the clearest statement yet of the modal approach Davis had been circling since *Milestones*: rather than running changes, the soloists improvise over sparse scales held for long stretches. "So What" opens on a bass-and-piano call and response before settling into a two-chord vamp; "Flamenco Sketches" is built on a sequence of five modes that each player moves through at his own pace.

Evans's voicings are the quiet engine of the date. His touch on "Blue in Green" is as spare as the melody itself, and on "All Blues" the 6/8 lilt he sets up gives Davis room to phrase behind the beat. Coltrane's choruses are denser and more searching, a hint of the direction he would take on his own Atlantic records the following year. Adderley brings the blues feeling the ensemble needs to keep the abstraction grounded.

Five stars. A session that sounds effortless and will be studied for decades.
