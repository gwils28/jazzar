Coltrane's first Atlantic album as a leader, recorded mostly in May 1959 with Tommy Flanagan (piano), Paul Chambers (bass) and Art Taylor (drums), is a compendium of the harmonic ideas he had been working out on the bandstand with Miles Davis. The title track's cycle of major-third key centers, taken at a punishing tempo, has already become a test piece for young saxophonists.

Not everything is a sprint. "Naima", named for his wife, is a ballad of suspended chords over pedal tones, and "Syeeda's Song Flute" has a nursery-rhyme simplicity that makes the leader's long, scalar runs sound almost playful. Flanagan copes gamely with changes that would unsettle most pianists; Wynton Kelly, who appears on "Naima", is more at ease.

Four and a half stars. The "sheets of sound" have found their architecture.
