Recorded in August 1969 and released by Columbia in April 1970, this double album is the most divisive record Davis has made. The ensemble is enormous — Wayne Shorter and Bennie Maupin on reeds, Chick Corea and Joe Zawinul on electric pianos, John McLaughlin on guitar, Dave Holland and Harvey Brooks on basses, Jack DeJohnette and Lenny White on drums, plus percussion — and producer Teo Macero assembled the final tracks from hours of tape with razor-blade edits and tape loops.

The title track and "Pharaoh's Dance" sprawl past twenty minutes, Davis's echoed trumpet cutting through a churning electric undertow. "Spanish Key" and "Miles Runs the Voodoo Down" are tighter and groove harder. Whether this is jazz at all is a question many readers will ask.

Three and a half stars. Compelling, uneven, and clearly the beginning of something rather than the end.
