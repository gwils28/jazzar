Mingus's Columbia debut, recorded in May 1959, is a tour of jazz history conducted by a man who refuses to stand still. "Better Git It in Your Soul" is a gospel shout in 6/8 with hand-claps and hollers; "Goodbye Pork Pie Hat" is an elegy for Lester Young, who died two months before the session, built on John Handy's grieving tenor; "Fables of Faubus" lampoons the Arkansas governor, though Columbia has issued it without the lyrics.

The band — Handy, Booker Ervin and Shafi Hadi on saxophones, Jimmy Knepper and Willie Dennis on trombones, Horace Parlan on piano, Dannie Richmond on drums — plays the leader's shifting tempos as if it had been born to them. "Jelly Roll" and "Open Letter to Duke" pay their debts explicitly.

Four and a half stars.
